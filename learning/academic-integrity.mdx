The platform helps protect academic integrity by identifying unusual behavior patterns across submissions and activity. When something looks inconsistent with normal learning, the system generates a Review Flag for the teacher to investigate. This approach supports honest assessment without creating an environment of constant surveillance.

## Signals That Trigger Review Flags

<CardGroup>
  <Card title="Unusual answer patterns" icon="activity">
    Answers that follow an atypical statistical distribution, such as every response being correct on extremely difficult questions while easier ones are missed.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Rapid completion" icon="zap">
    Assignments finished in a time span that is inconsistent with the length and complexity of the material. A student who completes a 20-question reading comprehension quiz in under 60 seconds may generate a flag.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Repeated identical submissions" icon="copy">
    Multiple students submitting answers that are character-for-character identical, especially on open-response questions, can indicate sharing of work.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Suspicious submission behavior" icon="alert-triangle">
    Patterns such as copying and pasting all answers at once, or submission timestamps clustered in ways that suggest coordinated activity.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Multiple accounts on one identity" icon="users">
    Indicators that several accounts may be associated with the same individual, which can suggest attempts to retake assessments or submit on behalf of others.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Large performance swings" icon="trending-up">
    Drastic changes in performance between assignments, such as moving from below-average scores to near-perfect scores without intervening practice or instruction.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## How Teachers See Flags

When the system detects one or more signals, it creates a Review Flag rather than taking automatic action.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Flag appears in the teacher dashboard">
    The teacher sees a notification that a student or assignment has been flagged for review.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Teacher reviews the details">
    The flag includes context about which signals were detected, such as completion time or answer similarity. The teacher uses this information to decide what to investigate.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Teacher takes appropriate action">
    The teacher decides whether to talk with the student, re-administer the assessment, or dismiss the flag if there is a reasonable explanation.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Info>
  Review Flags are prompts for investigation, not accusations. The platform leaves all disciplinary decisions to the teacher and school policies.
</Info>

## Quiz Safeguards

For assessments where quiz safeguards are enabled, the platform restricts certain behaviors to protect the integrity of the test environment.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Answer reveal disabled">
    When answer reveal is turned off, students cannot see the correct answer after submitting a question. Review of missed items is delayed until the teacher decides to release them.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Time limits">
    Teachers can set time windows for quizzes. The timer runs consistently and is visible to the student so there are no surprises.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

<Warning>
  The platform does **not** engage in invasive surveillance. It does **not** access your device camera or microphone, track your eye movements, or record your screen without explicit consent and a clear on-screen indicator. It also does **not** issue automatic punishments, zeros, or lockouts. All outcomes are decided by your teacher.
</Warning>

<Tip>
  If you finish an assignment unusually quickly because you already know the material well, your teacher can review the flag and dismiss it. The system is designed to catch patterns, not penalize genuine expertise.
</Tip>
