The platform tracks your learning progress across every subject, giving both you and your teacher clear visibility into what you have mastered and what still needs practice. Progress is presented as percentages, topic lists, streaks, and recent activity timelines. Teachers can view this data for individual students or across an entire class.

## What Is Tracked Per Subject

<CardGroup>
  <Card title="Progress percentage" icon="percent">
    A bar-style percentage that shows how much of the subject curriculum you have completed or demonstrated proficiency in.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Topics mastered" icon="check-circle">
    A list of topics you have successfully completed above the mastery threshold. These are marked with a check or badge.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Topics needing practice" icon="refresh-cw">
    Topics where your accuracy or completion rate is below the expected level. These are prioritized in your Practice For You recommendations.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Recent assignments" icon="file-text">
    A timeline of your latest assignments with scores and completion dates. This helps you and your teacher spot recent trends quickly.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Streaks" icon="flame">
    Your consecutive days of activity. Streaks encourage consistent practice and are visible on your personal dashboard.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Progress Bar Example

Each subject on your dashboard displays a progress bar that aggregates all tracked data into one visual summary.

```text
Mathematics        [████████░░░░░░]  53%
Topics mastered: 12 of 24
Topics to review: 4
Current streak: 5 days
```

The bar fills as you complete topics, master skills, and finish assignments. Gray segments represent remaining material. If you review a previously mastered topic and your accuracy drops, the bar may show a smaller decrease to reflect that the topic needs reinforcement.

## Student View vs. Teacher View

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Student View">
    You see your own dashboard with:

    <ul>
  <li>Personal progress bars for each subject</li>
  <li>Your mastered and pending topics</li>
  <li>Your assignment history and streaks</li>
  <li>Practice recommendations</li>
</ul>
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Teacher View">
    Teachers see a class overview plus individual profiles:

    <ul>
  <li>Class average progress per subject</li>
  <li>Per-student progress bars and topic lists</li>
  <li>Assignment completion and score distributions</li>
  <li>Review flags for academic integrity concerns</li>
</ul>
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

<Note>
  Progress is personal, not competitive. Your streaks and percentages are not ranked against other students. Teachers see your data to support your growth, not to publish leaderboards.
</Note>

<Tip>
  If your progress bar seems lower than expected, check your Topics Needing Practice list. Often the bar drops when previously mastered topics need a quick review session to refresh your status.
</Tip>

## How Mastery Is Determined

A topic is considered mastered when you meet both of these criteria:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Complete related assignments">
    You finish the required practice or assessment items for that topic.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Demonstrate consistent accuracy">
    You answer correctly above the mastery threshold, typically across multiple attempts or question types, not just once.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Mastery can be revisited. If you return to a mastered topic after a break and your accuracy drops, the system updates the status so you know it is time to review.
